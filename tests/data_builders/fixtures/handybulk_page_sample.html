Capesize Bulk Carrier Market – Baltic Capesize Index (BCI)

The capesize bulk carrier market maintained a firm upward trend throughout the week, with the Baltic Capesize Index (BCI) 5TC advancing from $42,105 to $48,399 as strength in the Atlantic capesize bulk carrier market combined with improving conditions in the Pacific capesize bulk carrier market. Pacific activity opened positively before experiencing a brief correction on Tuesday, but the setback proved temporary as persistent miner participation and operator-controlled cargoes quickly restored momentum. Baltic Capesize Index (BCI) C5 values subsequently moved above $16 for earlier arrivals, representing a substantial recovery from approximately $14.10 at the beginning of the week. The Atlantic capesize bulk carrier market provided the strongest contribution to the overall advance, particularly in the North Atlantic capesize bulk carrier market, where both transatlantic and fronthaul routes strengthened as fresh cargo demand emerged and progressively higher fixtures were concluded. East Coast South America (ECSA) and West Africa (WAFR) to China business also improved during the early part of the week, pushing Baltic Capesize Index (BCI) C3 values toward $37.90. Activity slowed temporarily on Thursday as bidding became more limited, although a relatively tight capesize bulk carrier tonnage position prevented any meaningful correction. Shipowners continued to maintain offers above $38, while charterers generally showed a preference for older capesize bulk carriers. Reports of business concluded at approximately $38.50 on more than one occasion on Friday restored confidence and left the capesize bulk carrier market with a firm tone heading into the weekend.

Panamax Bulk Carrier Market – Baltic Panamax Index (BPI)

The panamax and kamsarmax bulk carrier market recovered decisively during the week, with stronger conditions emerging across most major trading regions. The North Atlantic panamax and kamsarmax bulk carrier market moved from limited transatlantic enquiry to a considerably firmer environment, with charter rates rising by almost $2,000 per day. An 81,000 DWT kamsarmax bulk carrier open Spain was fixed for a voyage via Colombia East Coast back to Spain at approximately $20,500 per day. North Atlantic fronthaul business had already been showing greater resilience and strengthened further, with an 81,000 DWT kamsarmax bulk carrier open UK fixing via the US Gulf (USG) to Far East (FEAST) at around $32,500 per day. East Coast South America (ECSA) remained particularly active for second-half September positions, helping the Baltic Panamax Index (BPI) P6 gain approximately $2,000 per day during the week. An 82,000 DWT kamsarmax bulk carrier open Singapore was fixed via Argentina to China at around $22,000 per day. The Asian panamax and kamsarmax bulk carrier market recorded some of the strongest round-voyage gains, supported by North Pacific (NOPAC) grain demand, Australian mineral cargoes and firmer East Coast South America (ECSA) conditions. Earlier in the week, an 82,000 DWT kamsarmax bulk carrier open Japan was fixed via North Pacific (NOPAC) back to Japan at approximately $18,500 per day. Period activity also remained healthy, although some charterers remained cautious about committing before securing suitable initial employment. An 81,000 DWT kamsarmax bulk carrier was reported fixed for two years with delivery in Far East (FEAST) at approximately $20,000 per day.

Ultramax / Supramax Bulk Carrier Market – Baltic Supramax Index (BSI)

The ultramax and supramax bulk carrier market experienced a largely stable week, with limited overall movement and no clear directional change across the principal trading regions. The North American ultramax and supramax bulk carrier market initially appeared softer, but charter rates stabilized as the week progressed, with a 62,000 DWT ultramax bulk carrier open US Gulf (USG) fixed to Turkiye at approximately $31,000 per day. The Continent ultramax and supramax bulk carrier market remained steady despite relatively light trading, with a 62,000 DWT ultramax bulk carrier open ARAG fixing to Turkiye at around $21,500 per day. Conditions in the Mediterranean ultramax and supramax bulk carrier market were considerably weaker, with limited fresh enquiry and a shortage of Black Sea grain cargoes restricting activity. East Coast South America (ECSA) received some support from firmer panamax bulk carrier charter rates, although overall ultramax and supramax bulk carrier values remained broadly unchanged during the week. A 63,000 DWT ultramax bulk carrier open Argentina was fixed to China at approximately $19,850 per day plus a $1 million BB (ballast bonus). In the Asian ultramax and supramax bulk carrier market, charter rates edged higher gradually, supported by North Pacific (NOPAC) grain activity and improving cargo demand further south. A 62,000 DWT ultramax bulk carrier open Japan was fixed via North Pacific (NOPAC) to Vietnam at around $21,000 per day. Indonesia generated increased cargo volumes to India and Bangladesh, drawing additional tonnage from the Indian Ocean and helping tighten regional availability. The South Africa (SAFR) ultramax and supramax bulk carrier market remained largely unchanged, with a 62,000 DWT ultramax bulk carrier open South Africa (SAFR) fixed to China at approximately $24,500 per day plus a $250,000 BB (ballast bonus). Period activity was comparatively subdued, as much of the reported business had already been concluded during the previous week and charterers generally preferred to wait for clearer market direction.

Handysize Bulk Carrier Market – Baltic Handysize Index (BHSI)

The handysize bulk carrier market finished the week with a modestly firmer tone, although activity remained uneven and limited fixture volumes prevented a decisive market trend from developing. The US Gulf (USG) handysize bulk carrier market provided the strongest support, with tightening prompt tonnage, improving bids and increased cargo demand contributing to higher charter rates. A 33,000 DWT handysize bulk carrier open US Gulf (USG) was fixed to the UK at approximately $14,000 per day. Conditions elsewhere in the Atlantic handysize bulk carrier market were more mixed. East Coast South America (ECSA) benefited from improved cargo availability during part of the week, although sentiment weakened toward the close as fresh enquiry became less consistent. The Asian handysize bulk carrier market showed gradual improvement, supported by restricted prompt tonnage availability and continued regional cargo demand. Reported business included 36,000 DWT handysize bulk carriers securing regional and Pacific round voyages at approximately $15,000 per day. Despite the generally improved tone, overall handysize bulk carrier trading remained relatively light, leaving market participants cautious about whether the recent gains can be sustained.
